# What the acts do

Every state and territory has a security of payment act. It gives anyone who
does construction work under a contract a fast, statutory way to get paid:

1. The claimant serves a **payment claim** ([Payment Claim](/documents/payment_claim.md)).
2. The respondent either pays in full by the due date, or serves a **payment schedule** ([Payment Schedule](/documents/payment_schedule.md)) saying what it will pay and why it is paying less.
3. If the respondent pays less than its schedule, or gives no schedule in time, the claimant can go to adjudication, or recover the amount as a debt.

A respondent that misses the payment schedule deadline can end up owing the
**whole** amount claimed. Diarise every payment claim the day it arrives.

The acts sit beside the contract. They can't be contracted out of, and a
"pay when paid" clause is void.

# State by state

| State | Act (`[SOPA_ACT]`) | Builder to home owner? | Claim must say it's made under the Act? | Payment schedule due |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NSW | Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 1999 (NSW) | **Yes**, owner occupier construction contracts are covered (since 1 March 2021) | Yes. Include the statement on every claim[^nsw_sop] | 10 business days after the claim, or earlier if the contract says so[^nsw_sop] |
| VIC | Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 2002 (Vic) | **No**, domestic building contracts with an owner occupier are excluded, unless the owner is in the business of building residences[^vic_owner] | Yes[^vic_owner] | 10 business days (check after the 2026 reforms)[^vic_reform] |
| QLD | Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017 (Qld) | **No** for a resident owner's home[^qbcc_sop] | No. The claim must identify the work, state the amount and request payment[^qbcc_sop] | 15 business days, or earlier if the contract says so[^qbcc_sop] |
| WA | Building and Construction Industry (Security of Payment) Act 2021 (WA) | **Yes**, but a claim for home building work must come with the prescribed notice to the home owner | Yes[^wa_sbdc] | 15 business days[^wa_sbdc] |
| SA | Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 2009 (SA) | Check: residential work for an owner occupier is generally excluded | Yes[^sa_sbc] | 15 business days, or earlier if the contract says so |
| TAS | Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 2009 (Tas) | Check | Yes[^tas_cbos] | Check the Act |
| ACT | Building and Construction Industry (Security of Payment) Act 2009 (ACT) | **No** for residential work for the occupant[^act_mba] | Yes[^act_mba] | Check the Act |
| NT | Construction Contracts (Security of Payments) Act 2004 (NT) | Check | No set wording: a different model, where a "payment dispute" goes to adjudication[^nt_act] | No payment schedule step in the same form |

Including the "made under the Act" statement where it isn't required does no
harm, so the templates always carry it. Where the Act doesn't cover a home
owner, claim under the contract and the state's home building law instead,
with the [Progress Claim Invoice](/documents/progress_claim_invoice.md).

# Points that differ by state

- **NSW.** A head contractor claiming from a principal must attach a **supporting statement** (an approved form) declaring that subcontractors have been paid. There's a separate form for owner occupier contracts. Unless the contract says earlier, payment is due 15 business days after the claim for a head contractor and 20 business days for a subcontractor. One claim per reference date (the contract's date, or the last day of each month), for work in the last 12 months.[^nsw_sop]
- **VIC.** From 15 April 2026, reference dates and the "excluded amounts" rules were removed. Latent conditions and time-related costs can now be claimed, one claim per calendar month, up to six months after practical completion.[^vic_reform]
- **QLD.** Payment terms are capped at 15 business days for a head contract and 25 business days for a subcontract. Trust account rules (project trusts and retention trusts) apply to some contracts. Check with the QBCC.[^qbcc_sop]
- **WA.** The 2021 Act applies to contracts entered into from 1 August 2022. Older contracts stay under the Construction Contracts Act 2004 (WA).
- **SA.** A claim can be served up to 6 months after the work was last done.[^sa_sbc]
- **ACT.** A claim can be served up to 12 months after the work was last done.[^act_mba]

# Making a payment claim

- Use the [Payment Claim](/documents/payment_claim.md). Put the Act's full name in `[SOPA_ACT]`.
- Identify the work in enough detail for the respondent to assess it, and state the amount claimed. Run the numbers through the [progress claim computation](/computations/progress_claim.md).
- Serve it the way the contract allows, and record the date it was received.
- A [Tax Invoice](/documents/tax_invoice.md) can also be a payment claim if it carries the statement, which suits trades.

# Answering a payment claim

- Use the [Payment Schedule](/documents/payment_schedule.md), within the deadline above.
- Identify the claim it answers, state the amount you will pay (it can be nil), and give **every** reason for paying less. Reasons left out of the schedule may not be usable later.
- Back charges need a [Back Charge Notice](/documents/back_charge_notice.md) behind them.

# Not paid

Overdue amounts can justify suspending work (see the [Suspension Notice](/documents/suspension_notice.md)),
but the notice period and method are set by the Act and the contract. Get
advice first.
