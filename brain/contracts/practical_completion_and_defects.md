# Practical completion (PC)

PC is the point where the works are complete in accordance with the contract
except for minor omissions and defects that don't stop the home being used
for its intended purpose, and the required certificates are in hand. Each
contract defines PC in its own words ([clause map](/contracts/hia_mba_clause_map.md)).

PC usually triggers several things at once, which is why the date matters:

- It stops the clock on the completion date, and on any liquidated damages
- It starts the defects liability period
- It allows the final claim
- Possession and insurance risk usually pass to the client at handover

**Before issuing the notice**, walk the job with the
[practical completion inspection](/playbooks/practical_completion_inspection.md)
checklist. Then confirm the compliance certificates and the occupation
certificate (where the state requires one) are in hand. The notice states
the PC date, the contract completion date, the approved extensions of time,
the defects liability period, and a proposed handover inspection
([Practical Completion Notice](/documents/practical_completion_notice.md)).

If the PC date is later than the contract completion date plus approved
extensions of time, the builder may face a liquidated damages claim. Check
this before issuing the notice, and raise any outstanding
[extension of time](/contracts/extension_of_time.md) claims first.

# Defects liability period (DLP)

A period after PC (13 weeks is common in residential contracts; check the
contract) during which the builder must fix defects the client reports.[^beam_dlp]
Work out the end date with [add period](/computations/add_period.md).

- Log every reported item in the [Defects List](/documents/defects_list_tracking.md): date reported, location, trade, priority, date fixed and verified.
- Assess each item. Normal shrinkage cracking, wear and tear, owner damage and lack of maintenance are generally not defects.
- Fix genuine defects within a reasonable time and get the client to sign off each item.
- Give clients the [defects liability explainer](/documents/defects_liability_explanation.md) at handover. Its regulator list needs updating: in Victoria the Building and Plumbing Commission replaced the VBA in 2025.

# Statutory warranties

Separately from the DLP, state home building laws imply warranties into
residential building work. They last for years after completion, often with
a longer period for major or structural defects than for other defects (in
NSW, 6 years for major defects and 2 years for other defects). The periods
and definitions differ by state, so check the state before quoting them.
Home warranty or domestic building insurance may back these warranties if
the builder dies, disappears or becomes insolvent.

# Handover documents

Hand over, and record in the
[As-Built Documentation Register](/documents/as_built_documentation_register.md)
and the [Warranties and Manuals Index](/documents/warranties_manuals_index.md):

- As-built drawings, the survey and engineering certificates
- Compliance certificates: waterproofing, termite, electrical, plumbing, gas, glazing, smoke alarms
- The occupation certificate (where required) and the energy rating certificate
- Appliance and product warranties and manuals, and paint colour and finish schedules
- Keys, remotes and codes

# Final account and final claim

After PC, reconcile the job with the [final account](/computations/final_account.md):

- the contract sum
- plus approved variations, less omissions
- plus or minus PC and provisional sum adjustments
- less claims to date
- plus any retention released

Then issue the [Final Claim and Release](/documents/final_claim_release.md),
and keep the [Final Account Summary](/documents/final_account_summary.md)
workbook. The release does not end the builder's warranty or defects
obligations.
