> **Draft.** Contract formalities, deposit caps, insurance thresholds and
> approval names differ by state. Verify them for the job's state.

# The head contract (with the client)

- Use a written contract that meets the state's home building law for residential work. Industry standard forms (HIA, MBA) are written to comply in each state, but the edition matters ([clause map](/contracts/hia_mba_clause_map.md)).
- Record the key facts in the [Contract Data Summary](/documents/contract_data_summary.md): parties, contract type and edition, contract sum, deposit, site and title details, approvals, key dates, the defects liability period, the payment schedule, insurance policy numbers and the certifier.
- Work out the payment schedule amounts with the [payment schedule](/computations/payment_schedule.md) computation. It flags a deposit over the state cap.
- Put the agreed scope in a [Scope of Works](/documents/scope_of_works.md): inclusions by trade, exclusions, PC and PS allowances, the standards that apply, and the program. Have the client sign it as part of the contract.
- Before taking the deposit or starting, get home warranty or domestic building insurance in place where the contract value is over the state threshold. In NSW it is called home building compensation (HBCF).

# Before breaking ground

Work through the [pre-construction checklist](/playbooks/pre_construction.md):
approvals (development consent or CDC, the construction certificate or
building permit), engineering and soil reports, BYDA service locations,
erosion control, site fencing and amenities, the site safety management
plan, neighbour notifications and trade bookings.

# Subcontracts

Every trade gets the scope and price in writing before starting.

- A [Subcontractor Agreement](/documents/subcontractor_agreement.md) sets the parties, scope, dates, price, payment terms and core conditions: insurance, WHS, variations in writing, defects, back charges and termination.
- A [Trade Scope Confirmation](/documents/trade_scope_confirmation.md) confirms, for each package, exactly what is included and excluded, who supplies what (materials, fixings, plant, scaffolding, waste, power and water), and the program. Supply responsibilities are where most trade disputes start.
- Before they start: a current licence for the trade class, public liability (the agreement asks for $10M), workers compensation or personal accident cover as applicable, and a site induction. Track expiry dates in the [Subbie Insurance and Licence Tracker](/documents/subbie_insurance_licence_tracker.md).
- Security-of-payment law usually applies to subcontract claims. See [progress payments](/contracts/progress_payments.md).
- Paying ex GST plus GST requires the subbie to be GST-registered. If they aren't, the price is GST-free and their invoice shows no GST. Check their ABN.
