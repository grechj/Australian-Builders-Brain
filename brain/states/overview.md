Always ask which state the job is in before drafting a contract document.
These rules differ between states:

- Whether a written contract is required, and above what value
- Maximum deposit
- Cooling-off period
- Home warranty or domestic building insurance: threshold and when it must be in place
- Required form and signatures for variations
- Payment claim requirements and timeframes under security-of-payment law

# Who regulates what

| State | Residential building regulator | Main home building law | Security of payment law |
|---|---|---|---|
| NSW | Building Commission NSW (NSW Fair Trading) | Home Building Act 1989 | Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 1999 |
| VIC | Building and Plumbing Commission | Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995 | Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 2002 |
| QLD | Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC) | Queensland Building and Construction Commission Act 1991 | Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017 |
| WA | Building and Energy (DMIRS / DEMIRS) | Home Building Contracts Act 1991 | Building and Construction Industry (Security of Payment) Act 2021 |
| SA | Consumer and Business Services | Building Work Contractors Act 1995 | Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 2009 |
| TAS | Consumer, Building and Occupational Services (CBOS) | Residential Building Work Contracts and Dispute Resolution Act 2016 | Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 2009 |
| ACT | Access Canberra | Building Act 2004 | Building and Construction Industry (Security of Payment) Act 2009 |
| NT | Building Practitioners Board / NT Building Advisory Services | Building Act 1993 | Construction Contracts (Security of Payments) Act 2004 |

The building code itself (the [NCC](/ncc/editions.md)) is national, but each
state adopts it with its own variations and timing.
