> **Plain-English guide, draft.** It summarises the deemed-to-satisfy
> provisions for houses (Class 1) and their decks (Class 10). The NCC text
> wins: follow the clause links before you build, check your state's
> variations, and use the edition in force when the permit application was
> lodged. The figures below are the same in NCC 2022 and NCC 2025 unless the
> guide says otherwise.

# Do I need a balustrade?

Yes, along any side where someone could **fall 1 m or more** from the deck,
balcony, verandah, landing, stair or path to the surface below. Measure from
the deck surface to the ground (or whatever is beneath) at the edge.[^ncc_2025_hp]
Clause: 11.3.3 ([2025](/ncc/2025/housing_provisions/11_3_barriers_and_handrails.md#11_3_3), [2022](/ncc/2022/housing_provisions/11_3_barriers_and_handrails.md#11_3_3)).

A retaining wall doesn't need one unless it's part of, or right beside, a
path people use to get to the house.

# The balustrade itself

Clause 11.3.4 ([2025](/ncc/2025/housing_provisions/11_3_barriers_and_handrails.md#11_3_4), [2022](/ncc/2022/housing_provisions/11_3_barriers_and_handrails.md#11_3_4)).

| Where | Minimum height |
|---|---|
| Deck, balcony, verandah, landing, hallway, path | 1 m above the floor |
| Stair flight or ramp | 865 mm above the stair nosings or ramp floor |
| Inside edge of a stair landing, 500 mm long or less | 865 mm |

- **Gaps:** no opening a 125 mm sphere can pass through. On a stair, measure above the line of the nosings.
- **Fixed to the face of the deck:** the gap between the edge of the deck and the inside face of the balustrade can't be more than 40 mm.
- **Climbable rails:** if the fall is **more than 4 m**, nothing horizontal between 150 mm and 760 mm above the floor that a child could climb. Horizontal wires and slats are the usual problem.
- **Wire balustrades:** deemed to meet the 125 mm rule only if built and tensioned to the tables in 11.3.6 (wire diameter, spacing, post spacing and tension or deflection).
- **Glass:** glass balustrades must also meet H1D8 (glazing) as well as this Part.
- **Strength:** the balustrade must be designed for the loads in AS/NZS 1170.1. In practice that means a proprietary system with its installation instructions, or an engineer's or supplier's detail for posts and fixings.

# Handrails

Clause 11.3.5 ([2025](/ncc/2025/housing_provisions/11_3_barriers_and_handrails.md#11_3_5), [2022](/ncc/2022/housing_provisions/11_3_barriers_and_handrails.md#11_3_5)).

- At least one side, the full length of the flight, top at least 865 mm above the nosings.
- Continuous, with nothing on or above it that breaks your grip (newel posts and ball stanchions are fine).
- Not needed where the stair or ramp rises **less than 1 m**, on a landing, or on winders with a newel post.

# Stairs

Clause 11.2.2 ([2025](/ncc/2025/housing_provisions/11_2_stairway_and_ramp_construction.md#11_2_2), [2022](/ncc/2022/housing_provisions/11_2_stairway_and_ramp_construction.md#11_2_2)).

| | Riser (R) | Going (G) | 2R + G |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stairs (not spiral) | 115 to 190 mm | 240 to 355 mm | 550 to 700 mm |

- 2 to 18 risers per flight.
- Risers and goings constant through a flight: no more than 5 mm between neighbours, 10 mm between the largest and smallest.
- Open risers must not let a 125 mm sphere through.
- A worked check: a 1.2 m rise over 7 risers is 171 mm each. With a 260 mm going, 2R + G is 602 mm, inside the range. Run the numbers for every stair rather than eyeballing it.
- Stairs to attics and storerooms not used every day get a steeper table (11.2.2(2)).

**Landings** (11.2.5): at least 750 mm long, no steeper than 1:50, and
needed at a doorway when the stair from it drops more than 3 risers or
570 mm. **Thresholds** (11.2.6): a door sill more than 230 mm above the
ground outside needs proper steps.

**Slip resistance** (11.2.4): treads need P3 or R10 for dry conditions and
P4 or R11 for wet (outdoor stairs are wet), on the tread or as a nosing
strip, tested to AS 4586. Ask the decking or tile supplier for the rating.

# Attaching a deck to the house (NCC 2025 only)

NCC 2025 added a deemed-to-satisfy way to hang a deck off the house on a
**waling plate**: H1D11 and Housing Provisions Part 12.3
([H1D11](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#h1d11), [12.3.2](/ncc/2025/housing_provisions/12_3_attachment_of_framed_decks_and_balconies_to_external_walls_of_buildings_using_a_waling_plate.md#12_3_2)).
NCC 2022 has no equivalent: under it, attachment is designed (AS 1684 and
the engineer). The 2025 rules in short:

- Only for decks and balconies **no more than 3 m** above the footing, not cantilevered, carrying no walls, roof or another floor, imposed load no more than 2 kPa, outside alpine areas.
- The wall must be timber or steel framing, or 190 mm fully core-filled reinforced block, continuous to the footing with no openings or lintels below the deck.
- The joist span next to the wall can't exceed 3 m.
- Timber waling plate at least 90 x 35 MGP10 (or F5) on timber frames with No. 14 screws, or 140 x 35 on core-filled block with M12 anchors. Fixing spacings are tighter for tiled balconies than decks: follow 12.3.2(d) and (e) exactly.
- Fixings: **stainless within 200 m of breaking surf**, otherwise hot-dip galvanised, stainless or monel.
- Where cladding comes off, flash the top, bottom and sides of the plate (12.3.3) and reinstate the cladding.
- Decks more than 1 m off the ground need diagonal 30 x 0.8 mm galvanised strap bracing across the joists (12.3.4), with a second set if the deck runs more than 4 m out from the wall.

Joist, bearer and post sizes still come from AS 1684 span tables, the
supplier's span tables or an engineer. The brain's materials takeoff never
sizes them.

# Also check

- **Bushfire-prone land:** decks attached to a house must meet AS 3959 for the site's BAL. See [bushfire construction](bushfire_construction.md).
- **Termites:** a deck attached to the house is an "attachment" under termite management. See [termite management](termite_management.md).
- **Pools:** pool barriers are a separate set of rules (H7D2 and AS 1926.1).
- **Alpine areas:** external stairs and barriers also follow Part 12.2.
- **Timber and fixings:** see [timber treatment and grades](../materials/timber_treatment_and_grades.md) and [fixings and corrosion](../materials/fixings_and_corrosion.md).
