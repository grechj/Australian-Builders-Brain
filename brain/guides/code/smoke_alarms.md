> **Plain-English guide, draft.** This is what the NCC requires for new
> work. Several states also have their own laws for smoke alarms in
> existing homes (at sale, lease or renovation): check your state's fire
> and rescue or fair trading rules. Use the edition in force when the
> permit application was lodged. **NSW** replaces 9.5.1 and adds heat
> alarm requirements: read the clause page for NSW jobs.

Part 9.5 ([2025](/ncc/2025/housing_provisions/9_5_smoke_alarms_and_evacuation_lighting.md#9_5_1), [2022](/ncc/2022/housing_provisions/9_5_smoke_alarms_and_evacuation_lighting.md#9_5_1)).

# The alarms (9.5.1)

- To AS 3786.
- **Mains powered** where the house has mains power.
- **Interconnected** where there's more than one, so one sets them all off.
- In a garage where a smoke alarm would false alarm, another alarm type suitable under AS 1670.1 (usually a heat alarm) is allowed, as long as AS 3786 smoke alarms are elsewhere in the house.

# Where they go (9.5.2, a normal house)

- On every storey with bedrooms: in every hallway serving bedrooms, or if there's no hallway, between the bedrooms and the rest of the house.
- On every other storey, even with no bedrooms.

A Class 1b building (a small guest house or boarding house) needs one in
**every bedroom** as well (9.5.3), plus evacuation lighting that comes on
with the hallway alarm (9.5.5).

# Mounting (9.5.4)

- On the ceiling, at least **300 mm** from where the wall meets the ceiling.
- Sloping ceiling: **500 to 1500 mm** down from the apex or high point.
- If the ceiling won't work: on the wall, **300 to 500 mm** below the ceiling.

# On site

- Electricians: run the interconnect at rough-in (or use wireless-interconnected mains alarms where allowed), and test the whole set at fit-off.
- Renovations: adding bedrooms or a storey usually means bringing the alarms up to these rules. Check with the certifier.
