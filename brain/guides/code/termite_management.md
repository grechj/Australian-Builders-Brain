Part 3.4 ([2025](/ncc/2025/housing_provisions/3_4_termite_risk_management.md#3_4_1), [2022](/ncc/2022/housing_provisions/3_4_termite_risk_management.md#3_4_1)).

# Does the job need it?

Two questions (3.4.1):

1. **Is the site in an area with a subterranean termite risk?** Most of mainland Australia is. The NCC's explanatory note says termites aren't considered a risk in Tasmania and are a lesser risk in parts of Victoria. Ask the certifier or council if unsure.
2. **Are any primary building elements susceptible?** These count as **not** susceptible: steel, aluminium and other metals; concrete; masonry; fibre-reinforced cement; timber that's naturally termite resistant (AS 3660.1 Appendix C); and timber preservative treated to AS 3660.1 Appendix D.

If the whole structural frame is made of those (for example a steel frame,
or termite-treated framing timber, on a slab), the frame itself doesn't
trigger a system. Untreated pine framing in a termite area does.

# The options

Pick from the table in 3.4.2 for each part of the building:

| Building element | Options |
|---|---|
| Slab-on-ground, perimeter | Slab edge exposure, sheet, granular or chemical |
| Slab penetrations, joints and under the slab | Sheet, granular or chemical |
| Suspended floors | Sheet, granular or chemical |
| **Attachments** (decks, pergolas, steps, carports against the house) | A system on the attachment, or an inspection zone between it and the house |

The system must comply with AS 3660.1 (or have passed the AS 3660.3
Section 5 tests), and a chemical must be on the APVMA register (3.4.2).

# The durable notice

Fixed permanently somewhere obvious, such as the meter box (3.4.3), stating
the system, the date it went in, the chemical's life expectancy if one was
used, and the recommended inspection scope and frequency. Photograph it for
the handover pack.

# Carpenters and deck builders

A deck, pergola or step against the house is an attachment. Either keep an
inspection zone between it and the house so termites can't cross unseen,
or carry the termite system through to the attachment. Termite-treated or
naturally resistant timber in the attachment itself helps, but the
attachment can still bridge the house's system. See [timber treatment and
grades](../materials/timber_treatment_and_grades.md).
