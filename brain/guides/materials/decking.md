> **Guide, draft.** Board spans, joist spacing and gaps come from the
> decking maker's installation guide (and AS 1684 for the frame). Follow
> the maker's guide for composite boards in particular: its warranty
> depends on it.

# The main choices

| Board | Good for | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| **Merbau** (kwila) | Hard-wearing, stable, widely stocked in 90 x 19 and 140 x 19 | Tannin bleeds heavily when new: keep it off fresh concrete and render, and wash down before oiling |
| **Spotted gum, blackbutt, ironbark** | Australian hardwoods, very durable, good in bushfire areas (check the species and BAL) | Harder to fix: pre-drill near ends; check the grade (select or standard) |
| **Jarrah** | Durable, rich colour | Pricier in the east |
| **Treated pine** (H3) | Cheapest; easy to work | Moves and checks more; seal cut ends; needs regular coating |
| **Composite** (wood-plastic or capped) | Low maintenance, no oiling | Joist spacing is usually closer (check the guide), gaps for expansion along the length, hidden clip systems, gets hot in sun |

Common sizes: 70, 86 and 90 mm wide for narrow boards, 136 to 140 mm for
wide boards, mostly 19 to 22 mm thick, in random or set lengths (5.4 m is
common in pine). The brain's [deck takeoff](/computations/materials_takeoff.md)
works out boards, screws and frame from the deck size.

# Gaps and fixing

- Gaps between boards are typically 3 to 5 mm for hardwood depending on how dry the boards are (boards laid wet shrink, so they go in tighter), and as the maker says for composite. Gaps let water drain and leaves fall through.
- Two screws per joist; pre-drill hardwood near board ends.
- Screws: Class 4 or stainless in pine; stainless in hardwood near the coast and for oily species. See [fixings and corrosion](fixings_and_corrosion.md).
- Keep a ventilated gap under low decks: boards close to the ground with no airflow cup and rot.

# Finishes

- Hardwood: wash down (deck cleaner for tannin and mill oils), let it weather a few weeks, then oil. Re-coat yearly or as the oil maker says.
- Pine: oil or stain as soon as practical, and seal cut ends.
- Composite: no coating.

# Code points

- **Stairs and landing edges:** tread surfaces need P3 or R10 (dry) and P4 or R11 (wet), on the board or as a nosing strip (Housing Provisions 11.2.4). Ask the supplier for the rating; grooved or smooth faces rate differently. See [decks, balconies and stairs](../code/decks_balconies_and_stairs.md).
- **Bushfire-prone areas:** a deck associated with a house is covered by H7D4 and AS 3959. At higher BALs only some hardwood species, or tested non-combustible or composite systems, can be used, with rules on gaps and what's under the deck. See [bushfire construction](../code/bushfire_construction.md).
- **Termites:** a deck against the house is an attachment. See [termite management](../code/termite_management.md).
