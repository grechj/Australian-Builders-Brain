> **Guide, draft.** Board systems (fire walls, acoustic walls, bracing walls)
> are tested as a whole: build them exactly to the maker's system manual,
> with its board, studs, fixings and sealants, or the rating doesn't hold.

# Which board

| Board | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** 10 mm | Walls on studs at up to 600 mm | The default wall board |
| **Ceiling board** 10 mm (sag-resistant) | Ceilings | Use ceiling-rated board on ceilings; 13 mm for wider joist or batten spacing per the maker's tables |
| **Standard** 13 mm | Walls needing more stiffness or sound | Also used on ceilings per the maker |
| **Wet area** (water-resistant) 10 or 13 mm | Bathroom and laundry walls, as the substrate for tiles and membrane | Water-resistant plasterboard is an accepted wet area wall substrate (Housing Provisions 10.2.9); it still needs the membrane where the NCC calls for waterproof |
| **Fibre-cement sheet** (6 mm walls, 4.5 to 6 mm eaves, thicker for floors) | Wet area walls and floors, eaves, external | FC sheet to AS/NZS 2908.2 is accepted for wet area walls and floors |
| **Fire-rated** 13 or 16 mm | Garage-to-house walls, separating walls, around fire-rated systems | Only as part of the maker's tested system |
| **Impact-resistant** | Hallways, garages, rentals | Denser face and core |
| **Acoustic** | Bedrooms, media rooms, party walls | Higher density; usually with insulation and resilient mounts |

Brands colour-code the face paper differently: go by the printing on the
back, not the colour.

# Sizes

Sheets are typically 1200 or 1350 mm wide in lengths from 2400 to 6000 mm.
Order lengths that span the wall or ceiling with the fewest butt joints:
the [plasterboard takeoff](/computations/materials_takeoff.md) works out
sheets, screws, adhesive, compound, tape and cornice from the room size.

# Fixing and finishing

- Screws (and stud adhesive daubs where the maker allows) at the maker's spacing; glue-and-screw is standard on walls.
- Recessed edges for flush joints; back-block butt joints on ceilings.
- Three coats on joints: base (with paper tape), second, and finish (topping). Sand lightly, then seal with a plaster sealer before painting.
- Leave a gap at the floor so the board doesn't wick moisture; in wet areas follow the membrane system's detail.

See [wet area waterproofing](../code/wet_area_waterproofing.md) and
[membranes and sealants](membranes_and_sealants.md).
