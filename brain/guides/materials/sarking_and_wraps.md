> **Guide, draft.** Condensation rules differ by climate zone and changed
> between NCC 2022 and NCC 2025. **NSW keeps the NCC 2022 Amendment 2
> version of Part 10.8, and Tasmania the 2022 version.** Check the clause
> page for the job's state and edition.

# What they're for

- **Roof sarking** under tiles or metal: a second line of defence against wind-driven rain and dust, and (if foil-faced) part of the roof's R-value.
- **Wall wrap** behind cladding: keeps wind and water out of the frame while letting water vapour from inside escape.

The current worry is **condensation inside walls and roofs**: warm moist
air from inside condenses on a cold layer and rots the frame or grows mould.
The fix is to let vapour out and ventilate behind the cladding.

# The NCC's external wall rules (Housing Provisions 10.8.1, NCC 2025)

- A wrap or sarking used as a control layer must comply with **AS 4200.1** and be installed to **AS 4200.2**.
- Any layer between the cladding and the insulation must be **vapour permeable enough** for the climate zone (Table 10.8.1). In practice: use a vapour-permeable wrap in most of Australia, and never a foil vapour barrier on the outside of the insulation in cold climates.
- A wall with no wrap or water barrier behind the cladding needs a **drained and ventilated cavity**: at least 12 mm deep (battens), drained to the outside, with vents of at least 1,000 mm² per metre of wall at the top and bottom.
- Single-skin masonry and concrete, sandwich panels, and walls below ground are exempt.

# Exhausts and roof spaces (10.8.2 and 10.8.3)

- Bathroom and WC exhausts at least **25 L/s**, kitchens and laundries **40 L/s**, all ducted **outside** (not into the roof space), including vented dryers.
- In a bathroom without a window to the 10.6.2 standard, the fan is interlocked with the light and runs on for **10 minutes**, with make-up air (a door gap or vent).
- In climate zones 6, 7 and 8 (for example Melbourne, Canberra, Hobart and alpine areas), a roof with ceiling insulation (not insulation along the roof line) needs a ventilated roof space above it.

# On site

- Lap and tape wraps as the maker says, shingle-fashion (upper over lower), and tape around window and door openings with the maker's flashing tapes.
- Check the label: "vapour permeable" or "vapour barrier", and the water control rating.
- Don't let insulation push the wrap into the cavity or onto the cladding.
