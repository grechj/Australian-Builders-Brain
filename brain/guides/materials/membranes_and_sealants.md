> **Guide, draft.** A membrane is a system: primer, bond breaker,
> reinforcing, the membrane and its cure times, all from one maker. Use the
> maker's technical data sheet for coats, film thickness and cure, and keep
> it for the job file.

# Wet area membranes

For wet areas the NCC's deemed-to-satisfy waterproof materials are a
membrane to **AS/NZS 4858**, stainless steel, flexible waterproof sheet
flooring with waterproof joints, or waterproof sealant (Housing Provisions
10.2.8). In practice:

| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Liquid-applied, water-based (acrylic, polymer-modified)** | Most common for showers and bathrooms; brushed or rolled in 2 coats to the dry film thickness on the data sheet; long cure in cold weather |
| **Liquid-applied, polyurethane** | Tougher and more elastic; solvent smell; often for balconies and podiums (check it's rated for that use and under tiles) |
| **Sheet membranes** | Even thickness, quick to tile over; lapped and bonded joints are the weak points |
| **Preformed shower bases and trays** | Factory-made floor; walls and junctions still need the membrane |

AS/NZS 4858 classes membranes by how far they stretch (Class I to III).
The maker's data sheet states the class; match it to the substrate's
movement (timber floors and FC sheet move more than a concrete slab).

# Doing it right

1. Substrate clean, dry and sound: wet area board or render, falls already formed (1:80 to 1:50 to the waste).
2. Prime, as the maker says for that substrate.
3. **Bond breakers** at every wall-floor and wall-wall junction (a sealant bead or tape), so the membrane can flex rather than tear.
4. Reinforcing tape or puddle flanges at junctions and the waste; sealing collars at penetrations.
5. Two coats, to the data sheet's thickness, at right angles; let each cure.
6. Water-stop angle at the shower or room doorway where the detail calls for one.
7. Photograph each stage and flood test (where the maker or your state requires it) before tiling.

See [wet area waterproofing](../code/wet_area_waterproofing.md) for where
it has to go.

# External membranes

Balconies over living space, planter boxes, retaining walls and below-
ground walls need membranes made for them (UV, ponding water, or soil
contact). They aren't the same products as bathroom membranes: check the
data sheet lists the use.

# Sealants

| Sealant | Use | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutral-cure silicone** | Wet area movement joints (sanitary grade, mould-resistant), glazing, around fixtures | Paint over it; use acetic (vinegar-smell) silicone on metals, stone or mirrors |
| **Polyurethane** | Concrete and masonry joints, external movement joints, paintable joints | Use it in wet areas unless it's rated for that |
| **Hybrid (MS polymer)** | Paintable, bonds to most surfaces, wet or dry | Assume it's a membrane |
| **Acrylic gap filler** | Internal paintable gaps (cornices, architraves) | Use it outside or in wet areas |

Movement joints in tiling (perimeters, changes of plane, over substrate
joints) are filled with flexible sealant, never grout.
