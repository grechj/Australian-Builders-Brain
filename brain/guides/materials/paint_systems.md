> **Guide, draft.** The paint maker's data sheet sets the system, coverage
> and recoat times, and its warranty depends on them. These are the usual
> systems, to check against it.

# The system

Every painted surface needs the right **first coat** for what it is, then
two topcoats:

| Surface | First coat | Topcoats |
|---|---|---|
| New plasterboard and cornice | Plaster sealer (or sealer undercoat) | 2 x interior acrylic |
| Bare timber (inside) | Timber primer or primer undercoat | 2 x acrylic or enamel |
| Bare timber (outside) | Exterior timber primer; spot-prime knots with a stain blocker | 2 x exterior acrylic |
| Treated pine (outside) | Let it dry out first; prime as the maker says | 2 x exterior acrylic |
| Render, brick, concrete | Masonry sealer (alkali-resistant) once cured | 2 x exterior acrylic or texture coat |
| Fibre-cement | Sealer or undercoat to suit FC | 2 x acrylic |
| Galvanised steel | Etch or galvanised primer | 2 x exterior acrylic or enamel |
| Bare or rusty steel | Remove rust; metal primer (zinc-rich outside) | 2 x enamel or acrylic for metal |
| Previously painted | Clean, sand gloss; prime bare spots | 2 x to suit (test oil vs acrylic) |

# Sheen

Flat or matt for ceilings; low sheen for living areas and bedrooms;
semi-gloss for kitchens, wet areas, doors and trim; gloss for trim and
metal. Higher sheen is more washable but shows every flaw in the wall.

# Wet areas

Use a bathroom paint with a mould inhibitor on walls and ceilings outside
the shower; paint isn't waterproofing. See [membranes and
sealants](membranes_and_sealants.md).

# How much

Typical spread rates are around **12 to 16 m² per litre per coat** for
acrylics on smooth sealed surfaces, less on rough render, bare timber or
texture. The brain's [painting takeoff](/computations/materials_takeoff.md)
works out litres from the area, coats and spread rate (with 10% extra);
change the spread rate to match the data sheet.

# Decks and timber outside

Penetrating oils and stains (not film-forming paint) suit decks: they wear
rather than peel. Clean first (deck cleaner for hardwood tannin), let new
hardwood weather a few weeks, and recoat as the oil maker says. See
[decking](decking.md).

# Before you start

- Lead paint in homes built before about 1970: test before sanding, and follow your state's lead-safe work rules.
- Weather windows outside: no rain due before the paint is touch dry, and within the maker's temperature range.
