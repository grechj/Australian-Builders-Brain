## What is the defects liability period?

The defects liability period (DLP) is a set time after practical completion
during which your builder must fix defects in the building work. It is a
standard part of most residential building contracts and protects you as
the owner.

## When does it start?

It starts on the date of practical completion (PC): the date your builder
formally notifies you that the work is complete enough for you to move in
and use the home. Minor items still outstanding at PC are listed and fixed
during the DLP.

## How long does it last?

Your contract sets the period. Many residential contracts use 13 weeks
(about 3 months) from practical completion; some use 6 or 12 months. Check
the contract schedule.

## What is a defect?

Work that doesn't meet the standard required by the contract, the National
Construction Code (NCC), the Australian Standards or the manufacturer's
instructions. Examples:

- Cracking beyond acceptable tolerances
- Peeling or blistering paint
- Doors or windows that don't close properly
- Leaking taps or pipes
- Cracked or uneven tiles
- Drainage problems
- Incomplete or missing work

## What is not a defect?

- Normal building movement and minor shrinkage cracks, especially in the first 12 months
- Wear and tear, damage caused by the owner, and problems from lack of maintenance
- Natural movement of timber, minor colour variation in natural materials, and condensation

## How do I report a defect?

Tell your builder in writing as soon as you notice it, with:

- A clear description of the issue and where it is in the home
- Photos, if possible
- The date you noticed it

Email is usually best, because it gives you both a record.

## What happens next?

Your builder will inspect the items, confirm which are defects under the
contract, and arrange for them to be fixed within a reasonable time. Allow
time for trades and materials to be organised.

## Statutory warranties

Separately from the DLP, state home building laws give you statutory
warranties that last for years after completion. Often there is a longer
period for major or structural defects than for other defects (in NSW, for
example, 6 years for major defects and 2 years for other defects). The
periods and definitions differ by state: ask your builder, or check with
the state building regulator.

## If the builder doesn't fix a defect

Talk to your builder first, and keep everything in writing. If that doesn't
resolve it, contact the building dispute service or regulator in your state:

| State | Where to go |
|---|---|
| NSW | NSW Fair Trading (Building Commission NSW) |
| VIC | Building and Plumbing Commission (replaced the Victorian Building Authority in 2025) |
| QLD | Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC) |
| WA | Building and Energy |
| SA | Consumer and Business Services |
| TAS | Consumer, Building and Occupational Services (CBOS) |
| ACT | Access Canberra |
| NT | NT Building Advisory Services |
