### 6.3.1 Application

(1) [Part 6.3](), other than clause [6.3.4](#6_3_4), applies subject to the limitations set out in [H1D6(6)](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#h1d6).

(2) [Part 6.3]() need not be complied with if [H1D6(5)(a)](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#h1d6) or [(b)](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#h1d6) are complied with.

<a id="6_3_2"></a>

### 6.3.2 Structural steel members

(1) Structural steel members may be used as follows:

- (a) Bearers supporting a timber floor or non-*load bearing* stud wall — in accordance with [6.3.3](#6_3_3).
- (b) Strutting beams supporting roof and ceiling loads — in accordance with [6.3.4](#6_3_4).
- (c) Lintels supporting roof, ceiling, frame and timber floor — in accordance with [6.3.5](#6_3_5).
- (d) Columns — in accordance with [6.3.6](#6_3_6).

(2) Structural steel members in (1)(a), (b) and (c) must have a minimum nominal yield strength of 250 MPa.

(3) The yield strength of structural steel members in (1)(d) is nominated in [6.3.6](#6_3_6).

(4) Structural steel members described in this Part must be protected against corrosion in accordance with [6.3.9](#6_3_9).

### 6.3.3 Bearers

Structural steel bearers must comply with the following:

- (a) Effective bearer spacing must be determined in accordance with—
  - (1) for single span joists — [Table H1D6a](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#table_0f3a7032) and [Figure H1D6d](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#figure_6c7042eb); and
  - (2) for continuous span joists — [Table H1D6b](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#table_cf968a4f) and [Figure H1D6e](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#figure_cccaea2c).
- (b) Maximum acceptable bearer spans must be determined in accordance with—
  - (1) for single spans — [Tables 6.3.3a](#table_5c75a399) and [6.3.3c](#table_bd2eeca7); and
  - (2) for continuous spans — [Tables 6.3.3b](#table_db3bf5a6) and [6.3.3d](#table_46187dda).
- (c) All loads along the bearer must be evenly distributed.
- (d) The difference in distance between supports for continuous span bearers must not be more than 10% of the span.
- (e) Fixing of joists and columns to structural steel bearers must comply with [6.3.7](#6_3_7).
- (f) Bearers must be supported by structural steel columns that comply with [6.3.6](#6_3_6) and are fixed in accordance with [6.3.7](#6_3_7).

### 6.3.4 Strutting beams

Structural steel strutting beams must comply with the following:

- (a) Acceptable strutting beam spacing must be determined in accordance with—
  - (1) for single span rafters — [Table H1D6a](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#table_0f3a7032) and [Figure H1D6a](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#figure_5912a3ed); and
  - (2) for continuous span rafters — [Table H1D6b](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#table_cf968a4f) and [Figure H1D6b](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#figure_264f784f).
- (b) Maximum acceptable strutting beam spans must be determined in accordance with—
  - (1) for metal sheet roofs — [Tables 6.3.4a](#table_4605e461), [6.3.4b](#table_03e4c363), [6.3.4c](#table_b68629fa), [6.3.4d](#table_d543f6f0), [6.3.4e](#table_ec1b72ae) or [6.3.4f](#table_776980fa); and
  - (2) for tiled roofs — [Tables 6.3.4g](#table_51738198), [6.3.4h](#table_41c80f64), [6.3.4i](#table_beda506a), [6.3.4j](#table_04e33b21), [6.3.4k](#table_0d23199a) or [6.3.4l](#table_bf562ecf).
- (c) Any point load applied must be located within the middle third of the strutting beam.
- (d) Strutting beams must be tied down in accordance with [H1D6(3)](/ncc/2025/volume_two/h1_structure.md#h1d6) where supporting metal roofs.
- (e) Fixing and any cutting of strutting beams must comply with [6.3.7](#6_3_7).
- (f) Strutting beams must be supported by structural steel columns that comply with [6.3.6](#6_3_6) and be fixed in accordance with [6.3.7](#6_3_7).

- (b) The floor area to be supported is to be determined in accordance with [Table 6.3.6g](#table_b052994c) and [Figure 6.3.6a](#figure_21658c36).
- (c) The flooring system supported by structural steel columns must be fully braced to the footing level either by—
  - (1) subject to (d), adequately fixing the full height of the column to bracing walls of similar height in the two orthogonal directions of the building; or
  - (2) a bracing system designed in accordance with AS 1684.2, AS 1684.3, AS/NZS 4600, NASH standard or AS 3700 as appropriate to the materials being used.
- (d) For the purposes of (c)(i), the bracing walls must be capable of resisting racking forces in each direction not less than a proportion of the building’s racking force equal to the proportion of floor area that the column is supporting compared to the total floor area of the building.
- (e) Acceptable load eccentricity must not exceed 50% of the cross-sectional width plus 100 mm (see [Figure 6.3.6b](#figure_c9a06ac0)).
- (f) Have a minimum nominal yield strength of 250 MPa.

>
> The following is an example of the steps required to calculate a suitable column to support typical floor loads in a residential building. It is proposed the column will—
>
> - have an actual height of 1800 mm; and
> - support a timber floor only; and
> - be square in section; and
> - be cast in to the footings; and
> - be fully braced by column bracing sets.
>
> Step 1 – Determining effective column height
>
> The column height (H) is determined by multiplying the actual height by the relevant height factor (F1) in [Tables 6.3.6d](#table_6e624f71), [6.3.6e](#table_d7f97940) or [6.3.6f](#table_d75cefc7). In this case, the relevant value for F1 is 1.00 as the column is cast in to the footing and is fully braced.
>
> Therefore:
>
> - H = actual height x F1
> - H = 1800 mm x F1
> - H = 1800 mm (1.8 m)
>
> Step 2 – Determine floor area to be supported
>
> The column position selected is C4 as shown in [Figure 6.3.6a](#figure_21658c36) and the dimensions of L1 and L2 are 2700 mm (2.7 m), LA is 1900 mm (1.9 m) and LB is 2100 mm (2.1 m).
>
> The area supported by the column is determined by the formulae set out in [Table 6.3.6g](#table_b052994c).
>
> Therefore:
>
> - Total area supported (A) is 0.625(L1 + L2) x 0.625(LA + LB)
> - A = 0.625(2.7 m + 2.7 m) x 0.625 x (1.9 m + 2.1 m)
> - A = (0.625 x 5.4 m) x (0.625 x 4.0 m)
> - A = 3.38 m x 2.5 m
> - A = 8.5 m<sup>2</sup>
>

### 6.3.7 Fixings and bearing for structural steel members

### 6.3.9 Corrosion protection
