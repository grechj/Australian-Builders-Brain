From **NCC 2025 Volume Three**, Facilities and ancillary additions.[^abcb_ncc] Defined terms are in *italics*; see [definitions](definitions.md). State and territory variations (TAS) are shown in quotes beside the clause they change.

This Part sets out the requirements for a surface drainage system to a *Network Utility Operator's* stormwater system or an *approved disposal system*.

## Objectives

<a id="e4o1"></a>

> **TAS variation**: TAS replaces E4O1 Objective.
>
> The Objective of this Part is to—
>
> - (a) safeguard people from illness, injury or *loss* (including loss of *amenity*) due to the failure of a stormwater drainage installation; and
> - (b) ensure that a stormwater drainage installation is adequate; and
> - (c) conserve water and energy; and
> - (d) safeguard the environment; and
> - (e) safeguard public and private infrastructure; and
> - (f) ensure that a storm water drainage installation is designed and is capable of being maintained so that throughout its serviceable life it will continue to satisfy Objectives (a) to (e).
>

## Functional Statements

<a id="e4f1"></a>

> **TAS variation**: TAS replaces E4F1 Functional statements.
>
> Buildings and their surroundings are to be provided with a surface drainage and subsurface drainage installation and be constructed in such a manner as to provide protection for people, property and the environment from the adverse effects of stormwater.
>

## Performance Requirements

<a id="e4p1"></a>

> **TAS variation**: TAS replaces E4P1 Surface drainage systems.
>
> (1) A surface drainage system must have capacity to transfer the volume of water anticipated in an *annual exceedance probability* rainfall event.
>
> (2) The *annual exceedance probability* must be appropriate to the—
>
> - (a) importance of the building; and
> - (b) severity of potential damage to property, *loss* of *amenity*, illness or injury that would result from the failure of the system.
>

<a id="e4p2"></a>

> **TAS variation**: TAS replaces E4P2 Subsurface drainage.
>
> A subsoil drainage system must remove excess groundwater and reduce soil moisture levels to prevent loss by changing soil moisture conditions.
>

<a id="e4p3"></a>

> **TAS variation**: TAS replaces E4P3 Design, construction and installation.
>
> (1) A surface drainage installation must avoid the following:
>
> - (a) *Blockage* and *uncontrolled discharge*.
> - (b) Damage from root penetration, superimposed loads or ground movement.
> - (c) Entry of sewage and/or liquid trade waste.
> - (d) Entry of stormwater into a sanitary *drainage*.
> - (e) Damage to the *Network Utility Operator's* drainage system
> - (f) Foul air and gases accumulating in the stormwater system.
> - (g) *Loss* to buildings or property.
>
> (2) A surface drainage installation must ensure the following:
>
> - (a) Access for maintenance and clearing *blockages*.
> - (b) Stormwater is transferred to a *Network Utility Operator's* stormwater system or an *approved disposal system*.
>

<a id="e4d1"></a>

> **TAS variation**: TAS replaces E4D1 Application.
>
> Performance requirements E4P1 to E4P3 are satisfied by complying with E4D2 and E4D3.
>

<a id="e4d2"></a>

> **TAS variation**: TAS replaces E4D2 General requirements.
>
> A surface and subsurface drainage system must comply with—
>
> - (a) AS/NZS 3500.3; or
> - (b) [Housing provisions 3.3](/ncc/2025/housing_provisions/3_3_drainage.md)
>

<a id="e4d3"></a>

> **TAS variation**: TAS replaces E4D3 Protection from surface water.
>
> Surface water must be prevented from escaping a liquid trade waste generating area though the installation of a bund, speed hump or other feature a minimum of 150 mm high surrounding the area.
>
> > **Explanatory**
> >
> > Stormwater drains alone may not be adequate as stormwater will often flow over the grate and enter a liquid tradewaste generating area. The total surface water across the site is to be considered and the height of the bund or other feature should be increased where there is a risk of stormwater entering the liquid Tradewaste generating area.
